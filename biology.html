The four questions

1 · Did the runner run out of oxygen?

Lungs → alveoli → blood. Red blood cells carry O₂ to muscle cells, and CO₂ goes back to the lungs to be exhaled. Cells need O₂ for aerobic respiration in the mitochondria. Without enough O₂, muscles switch to anaerobic respiration (lactic acid fermentation), which makes much less ATP.

2 · Did the runner run out of energy?

Glucose holds chemical potential energy in its bonds. Cellular respiration releases it as ATP (usable cell energy), with heat released too. Blood glucose is kept steady by the pancreas: insulin lowers it (cells take in glucose, extra stored as glycogen), and glucagon raises it (glycogen broken back down to glucose).

3 · Did the runner overheat?

The hypothalamus (brain) keeps the body near 37 °C. Too hot means sweating and vasodilation: blood vessels near the skin widen to release heat. Too cold means shivering, which makes heat from muscle cellular respiration, and vasoconstriction: vessels narrow to keep heat in.

4 · Did the runner's water balance go wrong? → the answer

The runner drank too much plain water while losing salt in sweat. Blood sodium dropped below 135 mEq/L (hyponatremia). The blood became hypotonic compared to the cells, so water moved into cells by osmosis and cells swelled. Swelling brain cells caused the confusion and then the coma. Normally the kidneys, guided by the brain and antidiuretic hormone (ADH), adjust how much water leaves in urine. This is osmoregulation.

That's why sports drinks (water + salt/electrolytes) are recommended over plain water for very long exercise.

Trick: water moves toward where there is more salt, i.e. less water. "Hypo" sounds like "hippo": the cell swells up like a hippo.

Feedback loops

Negative feedback reverses a change and brings the body back to its set point, like the brakes on a car or a thermostat. Most body processes work this way.

blood glucose ↑ (after eating)→pancreas releases insulin→cells take in glucose, store glycogen→glucose ↓ back to normal
body temp ↑→hypothalamus→sweat + vasodilation→temp ↓ back to 37 °C

Positive feedback amplifies a change until an endpoint is reached, like pressing the gas pedal.

Childbirth: oxytocin → stronger contractions→baby pushes down→more oxytocin→… until birth
Blood clotting: platelets at a cut→release chemicals→attract more platelets→… until the clot forms
